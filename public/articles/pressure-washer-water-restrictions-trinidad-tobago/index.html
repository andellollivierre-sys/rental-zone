Can You Still Use a Pressure Washer in Trinidad and Tobago?

WASA's nationwide water-use restrictions took effect on November 16, 2026. Here's what the restrictions actually cover, what pressure-washer owners should check before starting a job, and how to enquire about renting equipment.

Quick answer: The current restriction prohibits using WASA-supplied water through a hosepipe or similar equipment, including a pressure washer, to water a private garden or wash a private motor vehicle. This is not a blanket ban on owning or renting a pressure washer or on every possible cleaning task.

What has WASA restricted?

WASA's nationwide restrictions took effect at 12:01 a.m. on Monday, November 16, 2026, and remain in force until further notice.

The restrictions cover the use of water supplied by WASA and drawn through a hosepipe or similar apparatus for:

  • Watering private gardens.
  • Washing private motor vehicles.

Pressure washers are included among the equipment identified in the public notice. The measures are intended to conserve available water supplies ahead of the 2027 dry season.

Read the report on the Government's explanation of the nationwide restrictions.

Does this mean pressure washers are completely banned?

No. Section 49(1) of the Water and Sewerage Act, Chapter 54:40, addresses specified uses of water supplied by WASA and drawn through a hosepipe or similar apparatus, including watering private gardens and washing private motor cars.

The current restriction should not be described as a blanket ban on pressure-washer ownership, rental or every possible cleaning activity. However, do not assume that another type of cleaning job or a different water source is automatically exempt.

Read the Water and Sewerage Act, Chapter 54:40 (Section 49).

What about driveways, walls and other outdoor surfaces?

The specific WASA restriction described above names private gardens and private motor vehicles. It does not, by itself, settle every question about driveway, wall, roof or commercial-property cleaning.

Before carrying out another type of pressure-washing job, check the latest WASA requirements for the activity and water source. Do not treat the absence of a named activity in a short public notice as automatic permission.

What should you check before pressure washing?

  1. Identify the water source. Confirm whether the water is supplied by WASA or comes from another source.
  2. Identify the job. Vehicle washing and watering private gardens are specifically restricted when using WASA-supplied water through a hosepipe or similar equipment.
  3. Check other cleaning work. Ask WASA about driveways, walls, roofs and commercial cleaning if the rules are unclear.
  4. Do not assume rainwater or tanker water is automatically exempt. Confirm the requirements for the source and activity.
  5. Reduce unnecessary water use. Sweep or dry-clean first where practical.

The Office of Disaster Preparedness and Management also provides drought guidance on conserving water and choosing lower-water cleaning methods.
