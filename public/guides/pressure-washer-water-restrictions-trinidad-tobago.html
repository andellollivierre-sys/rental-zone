Can You Still Use a Pressure Washer in Trinidad and Tobago?

WASA's nationwide water-use restrictions took effect on November 16, 2026. Here is what the restrictions say, what pressure-washer owners should check before starting a job, and how to arrange a pressure washer rental.

Updated November 27, 2026 · The Rental Zone TT

Rent a Pressure Washer Read the restrictions
Quick answer: The current WASA restrictions prohibit using WASA-supplied water through a hosepipe or similar equipment, including a pressure washer, to water a private garden or wash a private motor vehicle. This is not the same as a blanket ban on owning or renting a pressure washer, or a clear statement that every other cleaning task is prohibited. Check the rules for your specific job and water source.

1. What has WASA restricted?

WASA's nationwide restrictions took effect at 12:01 a.m. on Monday, November 16, 2026, and remain in force until further notice.

The restriction covers the use of water supplied by WASA and drawn through a hosepipe or similar apparatus for:

  • Watering private gardens.
  • Washing private motor vehicles.

Pressure washers are included among the equipment covered by the public notice. The measures are intended to conserve available water supplies ahead of the 2027 dry season.

Read the November 24 report on the Government's explanation of the nationwide restrictions.

2. Does this mean pressure washers are completely banned?

No. Section 49(1) of the Water and Sewerage Act, Chapter 54:40, gives the Authority power to restrict specified uses of water supplied through a hosepipe or similar apparatus, including watering private gardens and washing private motor cars.

The current restriction should not be presented as a blanket ban on pressure-washer ownership, rental or every possible cleaning activity. However, do not assume that a different job or a different water source is automatically exempt.

For driveway, wall, roof or commercial-property cleaning, confirm the applicable requirements with WASA before proceeding.

Read the Water and Sewerage Act, Chapter 54:40 (Section 49).

3. What should you check before pressure washing?

  1. Identify the water source. Confirm whether the water is supplied by WASA or comes from another source.
  2. Identify the job. Vehicle washing and watering private gardens are specifically restricted when using WASA-supplied water through a hosepipe or similar equipment.
  3. Check before other cleaning work. Ask WASA about driveways, walls, roofs and commercial cleaning if the rules are unclear.
  4. Do not assume rainwater or tanker water is automatically exempt. Confirm the rules for the source and activity.
  5. Use less water. Sweep or dry-clean first where practical, and avoid unnecessary washing.

ODPM also publishes drought guidance on conserving water and choosing lower-water cleaning methods.

4. Frequently asked questions

Can I use a pressure washer to wash my private car?

Not with WASA-supplied water through a hosepipe or similar equipment under the current restrictions.

Can I use a pressure washer to water my garden?

No. That use of WASA-supplied water is restricted.

Can I pressure wash my driveway or walls?

The specific restriction described above names private gardens and private motor vehicles. Do not treat that as automatic permission for every other job. Confirm the rules with WASA before proceeding.

Can I use stored rainwater or tanker-delivered water?

Do not assume that changing the source makes the activity exempt. Confirm the applicable requirements with WASA.

How long will the restrictions last?

They remain in effect until further notice. Check WASA's latest announcements for changes.
